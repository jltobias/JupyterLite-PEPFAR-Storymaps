# Research references

This bibliography collects research relevant to the PEPFAR StoryMaps project. The CRANE Survey papers describe populations and HIV-related experiences in Uganda; they are local evidence, not estimates of PEPFAR's causal impact. The PEPFAR studies cover program outcomes, policy, and funding disruptions. The current Map Tour's claims and locations are documented separately in [Map Tour sources](map-tour/SOURCES.md). Add a paper to an individual story or chart only when it supports that specific claim.

## PEPFAR outcomes and context

### Mortality and economic outcomes

1. Bendavid E, Bhattacharya J. (2009). [The President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief in Africa: an evaluation of outcomes](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-150-10-200905190-00117). *Annals of Internal Medicine*, 150(10), 688-695. Observational, cross-country mortality analysis.
2. Bendavid E, et al. (2012). [HIV development assistance and adult mortality in Africa](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.2001). *JAMA*, 307(19), 2060-2067. Observational analysis of adult mortality in PEPFAR focus and comparison countries.
3. Wagner Z, Barofsky J, Sood N. (2015). [PEPFAR funding associated with an increase in employment among males in ten sub-Saharan African countries](https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2014.1006). *Health Affairs*, 34(6), 946-953. Employment association study.

### Program and policy assessments

4. El-Sadr WM, Hoos D. (2008). [The President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief - Is the Emergency Over?](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMp0803762). *New England Journal of Medicine*, 359(6), 553-555. Perspective.
5. Palen J, et al. (2012). [PEPFAR, health system strengthening, and promoting sustainability and country ownership](https://doi.org/10.1097/QAI.0b013e31825d28d7). *Journal of Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndromes*, 60(Suppl 3), S113-S119. Review.
6. Institute of Medicine. (2013). [*Evaluation of PEPFAR*](https://doi.org/10.17226/18256). National Academies Press. Institutional report, not a journal article.
7. Cluver L, et al. (2023). [Reauthorise PEPFAR to prevent death, orphanhood, and suffering for millions of children](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01723-3). *The Lancet*, 402(10404), 769-770. Correspondence using UNAIDS estimates; the child mortality and orphanhood figures are descriptive and should not be treated as a stand-alone causal evaluation.

### Funding disruption: projections and observed services

8. Lankiewicz E, et al. (2025). [Early impacts of the PEPFAR stop-work order: a rapid assessment](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.26423). *Journal of the International AIDS Society*, 28(2), e26423. Early assessment of reported service disruptions.
9. Hontelez JAC, et al. (2025). [The impact of the PEPFAR funding freeze on HIV deaths and infections: a mathematical modelling study of seven countries in sub-Saharan Africa](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103233). *eClinicalMedicine*, 83, 103233. Seven-country scenario projections, including the 60,000-74,000 excess-death estimates for a 90-day freeze.
10. Stover J, et al. (2025). [Effects of reductions in US foreign assistance on HIV, tuberculosis, family planning, and maternal and child health: a modelling study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X(25)00281-5). *The Lancet Global Health*, 13(10), e1669-e1680. Multi-program scenario projections.
11. Honermann B, et al. (2026). [The Impact of the United States Foreign Aid Freeze on HIV Service Delivery in PEPFAR-Supported Countries: A Facility-Level Analysis of 2024-2025 Programme Data](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.70182). *Journal of the International AIDS Society*, 29(8), e70182. Analysis of reported program indicators; declines can reflect both service changes and missing reporting.

KFF's [mortality impact](https://www.kff.org/global-health-policy/assessing-pepfars-impact-analysis-of-mortality-in-pepfar-countries/) and [economic and educational spillover](https://www.kff.org/global-health-policy/assessing-pepfars-impact-analysis-of-economic-and-educational-spillover-effects-in-pepfar-countries/) analyses provide additional context, but are policy analyses rather than peer-reviewed journal studies.
